What the number means

Three reasons amplitude falls with travel time. The wavefront spreads over a larger area, so the energy per unit area drops. Some energy is absorbed as heat. Some is scattered out of the recorded direction by structure too small to image. The first is geometric and acts on all frequencies alike; the other two act more strongly on high frequencies, which is why a deep section is usually lower frequency as well as weaker.

t to the power n

One smooth curve over the whole record. Two events at the same time are multiplied by the same number, so their relative amplitude survives, and so does the comparison between one part of the line and another at the same depth.

An exponent near 2 corresponds to spreading in a constant velocity medium. What a line actually measures depends on what was already done to it.

AGC

Each sample divided by a measure of amplitude taken in a window around it, so the output has roughly the same strength everywhere. It makes weak deep reflections visible when nothing else will.

It also removes the amplitude relationship between shallow and deep, and between a bright zone and a dim one within the same window length. After AGC no statement about reflection strength is recoverable.

A legacy stack has usually been gained already

Archived sections were processed for display, and the sequence is often no longer written down. A line that measures a shallow decay exponent has already had most of it taken out, and applying a textbook t² on top of that multiplies the deep record by far more than the decay being corrected.

What the choice here settles for everything after it.

Which comparison matters on this line? Imaging structure needs the deep half visible and nothing more. Saying anything about how reflectivity varies with depth, or between two parts of the line, needs the amplitude relationship kept, which rules out AGC.

Does the measured exponent look plausible? Much below 1 on a raw stack usually means gain was already applied. The fitted line on the curve is the check: if the measured points curve away from it, one exponent does not describe this record and a value fitted over the whole of it is a compromise.

What does the deep half look like after? If gain brings up something structured, the later steps have something to work on. If it brings up an even texture with no organization, that is amplified noise, and the filters will happily shape it into something that looks like geology.

Is the muted zone being gained too? A t^n curve multiplies zeros by a large number and they stay zero, but AGC divides by a near-zero level and can turn the edge of a mute into a band of amplified nothing.
