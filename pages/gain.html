Amplitude

Reflection amplitude falls with two-way time through spherical spreading and absorption. A time gain restores it so that deep reflections can be seen and measured next to shallow ones.

How the settings are usually chosen

  1. The decay of RMS amplitude with time is measured on the input and fitted with a power of time. A time gain with that exponent flattens the curve.
  2. A time gain multiplies every trace by the same curve, so the amplitude of one trace relative to its neighbors is unchanged. The amplitude attributes remain comparable along the line.
  3. AGC divides each sample by the RMS amplitude around it. It brings up the deep record further, and it also evens out real differences in reflection strength within the window, which the lateral check below measures.
  4. Stacks from archives sometimes arrive with a gain already applied. A measured exponent near zero is the sign of that, and the step can then be skipped.
